# RecallReady: demo video script (narration, Kokoro am_michael, speed 1.0)

s1-hook: A supplier calls. The peanut butter on your shelves may contain Salmonella. Now the clock is running. Which lots do you hold? At which stores, in the warehouse, in online orders? Who has actually pulled it from sale? Which customers bought it? And can you prove all of that afterwards? Too often, the answers live in spreadsheets and email threads.

s2-intro: RecallReady is a Forge app for retailers, grocers and consumer-goods teams. It runs every product recall as a tracked case in Jira Service Management, keeps the catalog in Assets, and puts a Rovo agent on the team.

s3-catalog: Setup starts with one click. RecallReady builds its catalog in Assets: products with their barcodes and SKUs, lots, locations, stock and online orders. Here it is with a fictional retailer, Larkmere Market: twenty-four products, fifty-nine lots and ten locations.

s4-notice: A supplier email arrives. Paste it into Check a notice. RecallReady reads barcodes, model numbers and lot codes, and checks them against every product. Every match says why. The brand is named, every word of the product name is there, and the lot is listed: High. A similar product is only Medium, and brand-only matches stay folded away. Nothing is guessed.

s5-case: Start the recall. Seconds later there is a case, with one stop-sale task for every location that holds the lot, and the exact units to pull at each. The clocks of your response plan start running.

s6-confirm: Each store manager opens their task and confirms the units they pulled, with a note. Every confirmation is stored on its own task, so two stores can confirm at the same moment and nothing is lost. On the case, the team sees progress, effectiveness and the clocks, plus the online orders to contact, with a customer notice and a regulator summary ready to copy. RecallReady never sends anything itself. People decide.

s7-close: When every location has confirmed, close the recall. The closing record shows units recovered against units expected, every clock, and a full timeline. That is the evidence a regulator or an auditor asks for.

s8-mock: Food-safety standards such as the SQF Code require the recall procedure to be tested every year. A mock recall runs the real process on any product and lot, clearly labelled from start to finish. The yearly test takes one click.

s9-radar: RecallReady doesn't wait for the email, either. Every hour, the recall radar reads new FDA and CPSC recall notices. Its first scan on our site read three hundred and eighty-five notices from the last thirty days. A High match opens a case by itself. Anything weaker waits for a person to review.

s10-rovo: And the team can simply ask. The RecallReady agent in Rovo answers "can we still sell this lot?", lists the stores that haven't confirmed, checks a notice, drafts the customer notice, and opens a recall once the person confirms.

s11-arch: Under the hood, it is one Forge app. Assets holds the catalog, and work items hold the cases and stop-sale tasks. Around them: an issue panel, a recall board and a settings page, six Rovo actions with an agent and a reusable skill, a scheduled radar and a background queue. Work items are always read and changed with the person's own permissions.

s12-close: RecallReady. Every recall, tracked to the last unit.
